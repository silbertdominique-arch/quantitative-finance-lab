# Accounting Notes

## 1. The Accounting Equation

The fundamental accounting equation is:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Assets are resources controlled by a company that have economic value.

Liabilities are obligations that a company owes.

Equity represents the residual interest of shareholders after liabilities are subtracted from assets.

Therefore:

**Equity = Assets − Liabilities**

The accounting equation must remain balanced.

# 2. The Three Primary Financial Statements

## Income Statement

The income statement shows a company's financial performance over a period of time.

It generally follows this structure:

**Revenue
− Cost of Sales
= Gross Margin
− Operating Expenses
= Operating Income
± Other Income/Expense
= Income Before Taxes
− Income Taxes
= Net Income**

## Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows a company's financial position at a specific point in time.

The fundamental relationship is:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Assets can include cash, accounts receivable, inventory, property, equipment, and investments.

Liabilities can include debt, accounts payable, and other obligations.

Equity represents the shareholders' residual interest in the company.

## Cash Flow Statement

The cash flow statement explains how a company's cash changed during a period.

It is divided into:

* Operating activities
* Investing activities
* Financing activities

# 3. Apple Inc. — 2025 Form 10-K

Company: Apple Inc. (AAPL)

Fiscal year ended: September 27, 2025

Source: Apple Inc. 2025 Form 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Operations.

## Income Statement

### Revenue

Apple reported total net sales of:

**$416.161 billion**

This consisted of:

* Products: $307.003 billion
* Services: $109.158 billion

### Cost of Sales

Apple reported total cost of sales of:

**$220.960 billion**

Therefore:

**$416.161B − $220.960B = $195.201B**

This produces Apple's gross margin.

### Gross Margin

Apple's gross margin was:

**$195.201 billion**

Gross margin percentage:

**Gross Margin / Revenue ≈ 46.9%**

### Operating Expenses

Apple reported total operating expenses of:

**$62.151 billion**

This consisted of:

* Research and development: $34.550 billion
* Selling, general and administrative: $27.601 billion

### Operating Income / EBIT

Apple reported operating income of:

**$133.050 billion**

This can be viewed as a practical measure of EBIT for this analysis.

The calculation is:

**$195.201B − $62.151B = $133.050B**

### Income Before Taxes

Apple reported other income/(expense), net of:

**−$0.321 billion**

Therefore:

**$133.050B − $0.321B = $132.729B**

Income before taxes was:

**$132.729 billion**

### Net Income

Apple reported income taxes of:

**$20.719 billion**

Therefore:

**$132.729B − $20.719B = $112.010B**

Apple's 2025 net income was:

**$112.010 billion**

---

# 4. Revenue → EBIT → Net Income

The complete progression is:

**Revenue: $416.161B**

↓

**Cost of Sales: −$220.960B**

↓

**Gross Margin: $195.201B**

↓

**Operating Expenses: −$62.151B**

↓

**Operating Income / EBIT: $133.050B**

↓

**Other Income/(Expense): −$0.321B**

↓

# 5. Why This Matters for Finance

Financial statements are the foundation of financial analysis.

An investor can use them to evaluate a company's revenue growth, profitability, expenses, assets, liabilities, cash generation, and capital structure.

Investment bankers use financial statements when building financial models, performing valuation analyses, and evaluating transactions.

Quantitative finance also relies on accurate financial data when financial information is incorporated into models and research.

The key lesson is that financial analysis begins with understanding what the numbers actually represent.
